### Contraception

**Efficacy tiers (typical use):**

| Tier | Methods | Failure rate (typical / perfect, %) |
|------|---------|-------------------------------------|
| Most effective (LARC + sterilization) | IUD, implant, tubal ligation, vasectomy | <1 / <1 |
| Effective | Combined OCP, patch, ring, DMPA injection | 7 / <1 |
| Moderately effective | Diaphragm, condom (male), withdrawal, fertility awareness | 13-22 / 2-18 |
| Least effective | Spermicide alone, female condom | 21-28 / 5-21 |

**Long-acting reversible contraception (LARC):**

| Method | Hormone | Duration |
|--------|---------|----------|
| Copper IUD (ParaGard) | None | 10 yr (sometimes longer) |
| Levonorgestrel IUD (Mirena 52, Liletta 52, Kyleena 19.5, Skyla 13.5) | Progestin | 8 yr (52 mg), 5 yr (19.5), 3 yr (13.5) |
| Etonogestrel implant (Nexplanon) | Progestin | 3 yr |

**Combined hormonal (estrogen + progestin):**

| Method | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| OCP | Daily; many formulations |
| Patch (Xulane / Twirla) | Weekly × 3 + 1 off |
| Ring (NuvaRing / Annovera) | Vaginal; 3 wk in / 1 wk out |

**Combined contraception — non-contraceptive benefits:** ↓ menstrual flow / cramps, ↓ ovarian + endometrial cancer, regulate cycle, acne (some), endometriosis pain.

**Combined hormonal contraindications (US MEC Cat 3-4):**

| Condition | Restriction |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Migraine with aura** | Cat 4 — avoid |
| Hypertension uncontrolled | Cat 3-4 |
| Smoking ≥35 yr (≥15/d) | Cat 4 |
| **VTE / thrombophilia** | Cat 4 |
| **<6 wk postpartum + breastfeeding** | Cat 4 |
| **<3 wk postpartum any** (VTE risk) | Cat 4 |
| **Active liver disease, breast cancer current** | Cat 4 |
| ASCVD, stroke, ischemic heart | Cat 4 |
| DM with vascular complications | Cat 3-4 |
| SLE with antiphospholipid Ab | Cat 4 |

**Progestin-only options (for those who can't take estrogen):**

| Method | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| Progestin-only pill ("mini-pill") | Strict timing (3h window for older norethindrone, 12h for drospirenone); breastfeeding |
| DMPA injection | Q3 mo; **bone density loss** (reversible); delayed return to fertility (up to 9-10 mo) |
| Implant (Nexplanon) | Most effective progestin; up to 3 yr |
| LNG-IUD | LARC option |

**Barrier:**

| Method | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| Male condom | Only method with **STI protection** (along with female condom) |
| Female condom | STI protection |
| Diaphragm + spermicide | Fitted; UTI ↑ |
| Cervical cap | Less effective in parous |
| Sponge | OTC |

**Permanent:**

| Method | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| Tubal ligation / salpingectomy | Salpingectomy preferred — also reduces ovarian cancer risk |
| Vasectomy | Outpatient, more effective + safer than tubal |

**Emergency contraception (efficacy in order):**

| Method | Window | Efficacy |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Copper IUD** | 5 days | **>99%** — most effective |
| **Ulipristal acetate** (ella) 30 mg | 5 days | High |
| Levonorgestrel 1.5 mg (Plan B) | 3 days, less effective at higher BMI | Moderate |
| Yuzpe (combined OCP doses) | Older method | Lower |

**Switching / starting:**

| Scenario | Approach |
|----------|----------|
| Quick start | Begin any time if reasonably sure not pregnant; backup × 7 days for most hormonal |
| Postpartum | Progestin-only / IUD / implant immediately; combined after 21-42 days depending on risk + breastfeeding |
| Post-abortion / miscarriage | Immediate IUD or any method |

**Drug interactions reducing efficacy:**

| Drug | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Rifampin | Strong CYP inducer — backup needed |
| Some anticonvulsants (phenytoin, carbamazepine, topiramate, phenobarbital) | Backup; consider non-OCP |
| St. John's wort | Backup |
| HIV PI / efavirenz | Backup |
| Antibiotics (other than rifampin) | **Generally don't reduce OCP efficacy** despite popular belief |

**Common gotchas:**
- **Migraine with aura + estrogen → ↑ stroke risk** — switch to progestin-only / IUD
- **Postpartum + breastfeeding** — wait 6 wk for combined; progestin/IUD/implant ok immediately
- **DMPA bone loss** — reversible; consider duration; supplement Ca, vitamin D
- **Copper IUD = best emergency contraception** + ongoing
- **Ulipristal more effective than levonorgestrel** especially after 72h or higher BMI
- **No method (except condom) protects against STI**
- **IUD insertion** — pregnancy must be excluded; uterine perforation rare
- **Progestin-only pill** strict timing — easy to fail with delayed dose

**Rule of thumb:** Most effective = LARC. Migraine with aura, ↑VTE risk, smoker ≥35, postpartum <3 wk → no estrogen. Progestin alternatives + LARC. Emergency: Cu-IUD > ulipristal > LNG. STI prevention requires barrier.
