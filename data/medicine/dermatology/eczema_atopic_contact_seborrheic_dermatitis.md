### Eczema / Dermatitis

**Common eczematous patterns:**

| Type | Distribution / clue | Cause |
|------|---------------------|-------|
| **Atopic dermatitis** | Flexural (antecubital, popliteal); in infants face/extensor; chronic, relapsing | Filaggrin loss-of-function, Th2-skewed; atopic triad (eczema + asthma + allergic rhinitis) |
| **Allergic contact** | Distinct shape matching allergen (nickel, fragrance, neomycin, urushiol/poison ivy) | Type IV hypersensitivity |
| **Irritant contact** | Hands of healthcare/cleaners; from soaps, water | Direct cytotoxic |
| **Seborrheic dermatitis** | **Scalp**, eyebrows, nasolabial folds, chest; greasy yellow scale | Malassezia overgrowth |
| **Stasis dermatitis** | Lower legs, brown hyperpigmentation, varicose veins | Venous insufficiency |
| **Nummular** | Coin-shaped plaques, often legs / arms | Idiopathic, dry skin |
| **Dyshidrotic** | Vesicles on palms/soles, intensely pruritic | Triggered by sweating, stress, nickel |
| **Asteatotic ("xerotic")** | Cracking, "crazy paving" pattern in elderly | Dry skin |
| **Pityriasis alba** | Hypopigmented patches in children with atopy | Mild form |

**Atopic dermatitis — Hanifin and Rajka simplified:**

| Major (≥3) |
|------------|
| Pruritus |
| Typical morphology / distribution |
| Chronic / relapsing |
| Personal or family history of atopy |

**Severity-based therapy:**

| Severity | Treatment |
|----------|-----------|
| Maintenance / mild | Bland emollients **multiple times daily**; gentle cleansers; warm short showers |
| Mild flare | Low-potency topical steroid (hydrocortisone) for face / fold; mid-potency (triamcinolone) for body × short course; topical calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, pimecrolimus) for sensitive areas long-term |
| Moderate | Higher-potency steroid; **wet wraps**; consider phototherapy |
| Severe / refractory | **Dupilumab** (IL-4Rα), tralokinumab (IL-13), JAK inhibitors (upadacitinib, abrocitinib), cyclosporine, MTX, azathioprine |
| **Itch** | Sedating antihistamines at night (hydroxyzine, diphenhydramine); non-sedating less effective for atopic itch |
| Infection (impetiginized) | Topical or oral anti-Staph (cephalexin, doxycycline if MRSA suspected); bleach baths |

**Topical steroid potency ladder (selected):**

| Class | Potency | Examples |
|-------|---------|----------|
| 1 (super-high) | Highest | Clobetasol, betamethasone dipropionate |
| 2-3 | High | Fluocinonide, mometasone, betamethasone |
| 4-5 | Medium | Triamcinolone 0.1% |
| 6-7 | Low | Desonide, hydrocortisone 1-2.5% |

**Steroid principles:**
- Face / folds / genitals → low-potency only (skin atrophy, striae, telangiectasias)
- Limbs / trunk → mid-potency
- Palms / soles / lichenified → high-potency
- Limit duration; taper into lower potency / non-steroidal

**Contact dermatitis workup:**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| History — what touches the area | Job, hobby, jewelry, fragrance, plants, medications |
| Distribution clue | Watchband (nickel), earring back (nickel), shoe (rubber), lip-licker (food/lip balm) |
| **Patch test** for allergic contact | Confirms allergen; T-cell delayed response |
| Avoidance | Cornerstone |
| Topical steroid | Acute flare |

**Seborrheic dermatitis treatment:**

| Site | Therapy |
|------|---------|
| Scalp | Selenium sulfide, zinc pyrithione, ketoconazole shampoo; mid-potency steroid solution short-term |
| Face | Ketoconazole cream + low-potency steroid short-term; calcineurin inhibitor for long-term |
| Adult onset / severe / persistent → consider **HIV, Parkinson disease** |

**Stasis dermatitis:**

| Treatment | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Compression | Cornerstone (rule out arterial insufficiency: ABI ≥0.8) |
| Leg elevation | — |
| Topical steroid | Brief course for acute flare |
| Treat venous insufficiency | Long-term |
| **Don't mistake for cellulitis** | Bilateral, chronic, not warm/painful → no antibiotics |

**Common gotchas:**
- **"Cellulitis" that doesn't respond to antibiotics** in elderly — often stasis dermatitis
- **Nickel allergy** is the most common contact allergen; common in jewelry, jeans buttons, watchbands, cell phones
- **Topical steroid** on face long-term → atrophy, perioral dermatitis, rebound
- **Seborrheic dermatitis sudden onset / severe** → screen HIV
- **Dupilumab** can cause **conjunctivitis** in atopic patients
- **Wet wraps** are very effective for severe atopic flares
- **Bleach baths** (¼ cup in full bathtub, 2× weekly) reduce S. aureus colonization in atopics

**Rule of thumb:** Distribution suggests cause. Atopic = flexural + atopic triad → emollient + topical steroid → biologic. Allergic contact = patch test + avoid. Seborrheic = antifungal + low-dose steroid; severe → think HIV/Parkinson. Stasis ≠ cellulitis.
