### End-of-Life Care, Advance Directives, DNR

**Advance directive types:**

| Type | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Living will** | Patient's written instructions about treatments they want / don't want if incapacitated |
| **Durable Power of Attorney for Healthcare (DPOAHC) / Healthcare Proxy** | Names a surrogate decision-maker |
| **POLST / MOLST** | Physician orders for life-sustaining treatment — actionable medical orders; for seriously ill / frail; portable across settings |
| **DNR / DNI** | Specific orders; often a subset within POLST |

**Hierarchy of decision-making:**

| Order | Source |
|-------|--------|
| Patient with capacity | Always primary |
| Healthcare proxy / DPOA (named) | Next |
| State default surrogate hierarchy (spouse → adult children → parents → siblings → other relative or close friend) | Next |
| Court-appointed guardian | Last resort |

**Substituted judgment vs best interest:**

| Standard | Use |
|----------|-----|
| Substituted judgment | "What would the patient want based on prior wishes / values?" — preferred |
| Best interest | When patient's wishes unknown |

**Code status discussions — common terms:**

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Full code** | All measures including CPR, intubation, defibrillation |
| **DNR (do not resuscitate)** | No CPR / defibrillation if pulseless |
| **DNI (do not intubate)** | No intubation/mechanical ventilation; **but other measures (drugs, ICU) okay** |
| **Allow Natural Death (AND)** | Synonym for DNR/DNI in many institutions |
| **Comfort care only** | Symptom management; usually withdraw life-sustaining; transition to hospice |
| **Limited resuscitation** | Specific subset (e.g., chest compressions but no intubation) — **avoid as it's confusing** |

**DNR ≠ "do not treat":**
- Patients with DNR still receive antibiotics, blood, fluids, medications, surgery as appropriate
- Not "withdraw all"

**Withholding vs withdrawing — ethically equivalent:**

| Item |
|------|
| Both are ethical when consistent with patient wishes / best interest |
| Withdrawing emotionally harder for clinicians and families |
| Patients who try a treatment and find it not effective have right to discontinue (and surrogate can decide) |

**Doctrine of double effect:**

| Criteria |
|----------|
| Action itself good or neutral (giving pain medication) |
| Bad effect (respiratory depression, hastening death) is foreseen but not intended |
| Bad effect not the means to the good effect |
| Proportionate reason for tolerating bad effect |

Allows aggressive symptom management at end of life.

**Palliative sedation:**

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Goal | Relieve refractory symptoms (pain, dyspnea, delirium, agitation) |
| Method | Sedating medications (midazolam, propofol, phenobarbital) |
| Not the same as euthanasia | Intent matters; titrate to symptom relief |
| Typically for terminal patients with hours-to-days prognosis |

**Hospice vs palliative care:**

| | Hospice | Palliative care |
|---|---------|-----------------|
| Eligibility | Terminal, prognosis ≤6 months, forgoing curative treatment (US Medicare) | Any serious illness, any stage, alongside curative |
| Setting | Home, hospice facility, nursing facility, hospital | Anywhere |
| Focus | Comfort | Symptom management + advance care planning + curative if desired |

**Voluntary stopping eating and drinking (VSED):**

| Item |
|------|
| Capacitated patient chooses to stop |
| Death typically in 1-2 weeks |
| Permitted in most jurisdictions |
| Hospice / palliative often supports symptomatically |

**Medical aid in dying (MAID):**

| US states (varies) | Detail |
|--------------------|--------|
| Oregon (1997 first), Washington, Vermont, California, Hawaii, NJ, NM, NY, ME, NV, others | Specific requirements: terminal (≤6 mo), capacity, voluntary, residency (some), waiting periods, multiple requests, written, two physicians |
| Globally | Belgium, Netherlands, Canada (MAID), others — broader criteria in some |

Active euthanasia (clinician administering lethal medication) is illegal in US; legal in some countries.

**Brain death:**

| Criteria (AAN, 2023 update) |
|------------------------------|
| Coma with known cause |
| **Absent brainstem reflexes** (pupillary, corneal, oculocephalic, oculovestibular, gag, cough) |
| **Apnea test** (no spontaneous respirations after PaCO2 rises ≥20 from baseline or ≥60) |
| Confounders excluded (hypothermia, sedation, severe metabolic, paralytic) |
| Confirmatory tests if needed (EEG, cerebral blood flow, TCD) |

Brain death = legal death in US. Organ donation discussions follow.

**Determination of death by circulatory criteria** — irreversible cessation of circulation and respiration. After 5 min of asystole post-extubation in donation after circulatory death (DCD).

**Common scenarios — practical:**

| Scenario | Approach |
|----------|----------|
| Family wants "everything" but patient is dying | Reframe: "What do you mean by everything?" Discuss patient's values, prognosis, treatments that won't help |
| Conflict between family members | Family meeting, ethics consult, identify legal surrogate |
| Patient's stated wishes conflict with surrogate now | Patient's prior wishes (advance directive) generally override |
| New diagnosis / change in condition | Re-discuss code status |
| Pediatric end-of-life | Parents primary; **assent** from capable child; multidisciplinary support |
| Cultural / religious preferences | Respect; involve chaplain / clergy; may shape preferences for hydration, autopsy, organ donation |
| Family asks "Don't tell mom" | Understand reason; explore patient's preferences for information; cultural sensitivity vs autonomy tension |

**Withdrawal of life support — practical steps:**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Family meeting | Discuss prognosis, wishes, plan |
| Ethics / palliative consult | If conflict / complex |
| Optimize comfort measures first | Opioids, benzodiazepines |
| **Discontinue interventions** | Stop pressors, ventilator (extubation or terminal weaning), dialysis, etc. |
| Continue comfort care | Pain, dyspnea, secretions, agitation management |
| Family support | Chaplain, social work, presence |
| Document discussion + decisions | — |

**Common gotchas:**
- **Patient's living will applies only when incapacitated** — capacitated patient overrides any prior document
- **DNR doesn't mean withdraw care** — emphasize this with patients/families
- **Brain death is legal death** — different from "vegetative state" or "minimally conscious"
- **Hospice eligibility ≠ giving up** — it's appropriate care at the end of life
- **Cultural sensitivity** — some cultures defer to family head, view explicit prognosis discussion as harmful
- **Disclose medical errors** — ethically required even if uncomfortable
- **Ethics consult** is a resource, not adversarial — use early in conflict
- **Document** all conversations clearly — what was discussed, by whom, decisions made

**Rule of thumb:** Patient with capacity > advance directive > surrogate. DNR = no CPR, not "no care". Withhold ≡ withdraw ethically. Double effect permits aggressive symptom relief. Brain death = legal death. When unsure, palliative + ethics consult.
