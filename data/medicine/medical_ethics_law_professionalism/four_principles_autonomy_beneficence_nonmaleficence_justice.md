### Four Principles of Medical Ethics

**Beauchamp & Childress framework:**

| Principle | Definition | Practical |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Autonomy** | Respect patient's right to make own informed decisions | Informed consent, capacity assessment, refusing treatment, advance directives |
| **Beneficence** | Act in patient's best interest | Recommend evidence-based care, not unwanted intervention |
| **Non-maleficence** | "First, do no harm" | Avoid procedures with unfavorable risk/benefit; informed risk discussion |
| **Justice** | Fair distribution of benefits, risks, and resources | Equitable access, allocation, non-discrimination |

**Tensions and trade-offs (the work of ethics):**

| Conflict | Example |
|----------|---------|
| Autonomy vs beneficence | Patient refuses life-saving transfusion (Jehovah's Witness) |
| Autonomy vs non-maleficence | Patient demands antibiotic for viral illness |
| Beneficence vs justice | Resource allocation in scarcity (ICU beds, transplants) |
| Confidentiality vs harm prevention | Tarasoff: warning identifiable victim of patient's threat |

**Informed consent — required elements:**

| Element |
|---------|
| **Disclosure** of diagnosis, prognosis, proposed intervention, alternatives (including no treatment), risks, benefits |
| **Comprehension** by patient |
| **Voluntariness** (no coercion) |
| **Capacity** to decide |
| Documentation |

**Capacity (decision-specific, can fluctuate):**

| Component | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| Communicate a choice | "What do you want?" |
| Understand information | "Tell me what you heard" |
| Appreciate situation | "What does this mean for you?" |
| Reasoning | "How did you decide?" |

Capacity ≠ competency (capacity is clinical judgment; competency is a legal designation).

**Surrogate decision-making hierarchy (US, varies by state):**

| Order |
|-------|
| 1. Healthcare proxy / DPOA (durable power of attorney for healthcare) |
| 2. Spouse |
| 3. Adult children (consensus) |
| 4. Parents |
| 5. Adult siblings |
| 6. Other relative or close friend |
| 7. Court-appointed guardian |

**Standards for surrogate decisions:**

| Standard | Use |
|----------|-----|
| **Substituted judgment** | "What would the patient have wanted?" Use when patient's prior wishes known |
| **Best interest** | When prior wishes unknown |

**Advance directives:**

| Type | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Living will** | Specific treatment wishes (e.g., "no intubation, no CPR if terminal") |
| **DPOA / healthcare proxy** | Names a person to decide |
| **POLST / MOLST** | Physician orders for life-sustaining treatment — actionable medical order, often for seriously ill |
| **DNR / DNI** | Do not resuscitate / do not intubate — usually a written order |

**Confidentiality — when can / must you break it:**

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| **Tarasoff duty** | Identifiable specific threat to identifiable third party — warn / protect |
| Reportable infectious diseases | TB, gonorrhea, syphilis, HIV (varies), measles, etc. |
| **Suspected abuse** | Children (mandatory), dependent adults, elderly |
| Gunshot / stab wound | Most states |
| Driving impairment (seizures, dementia) | Varies by state |
| Court order / subpoena | With process |
| Imminent self-harm | Hospitalize involuntarily |

**Special situations:**

| Scenario | Approach |
|----------|----------|
| **Minors** | Parental consent generally; **mature minor doctrine** (some states for ages 14+); emancipation; adolescent confidentiality (sex, contraception, mental health, substance use varies by state) |
| Emergency without consent | Implied consent — proceed with reasonable lifesaving treatment |
| Pregnancy + maternal-fetal conflict | Generally favor maternal autonomy; ethics consult |
| Jehovah's Witness refusing transfusion | Adult with capacity — autonomy; minor — court order overriding parents in life-threatening |
| Suicidal patient refusing treatment | Capacity often impaired by illness — involuntary hold for evaluation |
| Disclosure of medical error | **Disclose** — ethically required, legally protected in some states ("apology laws") |

**End-of-life concepts:**

| Concept | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Withholding vs withdrawing** | Ethically equivalent; psychologically harder to withdraw |
| **Double effect** | Acceptable to give pain medication knowing it may shorten life, **if** intent is symptom relief, not death |
| **Voluntary stopping eating and drinking (VSED)** | Permitted in capacitated patients |
| **Medical aid in dying (MAID)** | Legal in some US states (Oregon, etc.) and countries; specific requirements |
| **Active euthanasia** | Illegal in US (legal in some countries) |
| Palliative sedation | Sedate to relieve refractory symptoms; not aimed at hastening death |
| **Brain death** | Legally equivalent to death; specific testing protocols |

**Conflicts of interest, gifts:**

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Pharma gifts | Generally avoid significant gifts; small educational items often acceptable per institution |
| Self-referral | Disclose financial interest |
| Industry payments | Public reporting (Sunshine Act) |

**Disclosure of poor prognosis / breaking bad news (SPIKES):**

| Letter | Step |
|--------|------|
| **S** | Setting (private, time, sit, no interruptions) |
| **P** | Perception (what does the patient know?) |
| **I** | Invitation (how much do they want to know?) |
| **K** | Knowledge (give information clearly) |
| **E** | Emotions (acknowledge, empathize) |
| **S** | Summarize, plan next steps |

**Common gotchas:**
- **Capacity is decision-specific** — patient may have capacity to refuse one thing but not another
- **Adolescents and confidentiality** — some areas (sex, contraception, mental health, substance use) are protected from parental disclosure depending on state
- **Family members may not be the legal surrogate** — proxy / DPOA outranks spouse if explicitly designated
- **DNR ≠ comfort care only** — DNR means no CPR, not "no treatment"
- **Withholding = withdrawing ethically** — courts and ethics agree, but emotional weight differs
- **HIPAA exceptions exist** for public health, abuse, court orders, imminent threat
- **Conflict between patient + family** — patient's wishes prevail if patient has capacity
- **Don't promise complete confidentiality to adolescents** — explain mandatory exceptions up front
- **Document capacity assessment** clearly

**Rule of thumb:** Four principles balance: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice. Capacity is decision-specific. Use substituted judgment > best interest. Living will + healthcare proxy guide. Disclosure exceptions: harm to identifiable other, abuse, public health. SPIKES for bad news.
