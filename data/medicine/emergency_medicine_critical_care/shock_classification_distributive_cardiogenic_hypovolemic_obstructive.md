### Shock — Classification & Initial Management

**Four major shock states (hemodynamic patterns):**

| Type | Cardiac output | SVR | CVP / preload | Wedge / LVEDP | Mixed venous SatO2 |
|------|---------------|-----|---------------|---------------|--------------------|
| **Hypovolemic** | ↓ | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ |
| **Cardiogenic** | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |
| **Obstructive** | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ (RV strain) | Variable | ↓ |
| **Distributive** | ↑ or normal (early) | **↓** | ↓ or normal | ↓ or normal | **↑** (poor extraction) |

Septic shock = distributive most common; anaphylactic, neurogenic, adrenal also distributive.

**Causes by category:**

| Type | Causes |
|------|--------|
| Hypovolemic | Hemorrhage, GI losses, burns, third-spacing |
| Cardiogenic | MI (large), HF decompensated, arrhythmia, valvular failure (acute MR/AR), myocarditis, cardiomyopathy |
| **Obstructive** | **Tension pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade, massive PE, severe AS** |
| Distributive | Sepsis, anaphylaxis, neurogenic (high spinal cord injury), adrenal insufficiency, post-cardiac arrest |

**Clinical clues:**

| Type | Skin | JVP | Lungs |
|------|------|-----|-------|
| Hypovolemic | Cold, dry | Flat | Clear |
| Cardiogenic | Cold, clammy | ↑ | Crackles, edema |
| Obstructive (tamponade) | Cold | ↑↑ pulsus paradoxus | Clear |
| Distributive (early septic) | **Warm**, flushed | Flat | Variable |

**Resuscitation framework:**

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | ABCs, IV access × 2 large-bore, monitor, O2 |
| 2 | **Identify type** (hypotension + lactate + hemodynamic clues) |
| 3 | **Targeted volume**: liberal in hypovolemic / distributive; cautious in cardiogenic; depends on obstructive cause |
| 4 | **Vasopressors** if MAP <65 not responsive |
| 5 | Source control / definitive treatment |

**Vasopressor / inotrope cheat sheet:**

| Drug | Receptors | Dominant effect | Use |
|------|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| Norepinephrine | α > β1 | Vasoconstriction + mild inotropy | **First-line septic shock**, most distributive |
| Vasopressin | V1 | Pure vasoconstriction (V1 vascular) | Add-on to norepi in septic shock |
| Epinephrine | α + β1 + β2 | Inotropy + vasoconstriction | Anaphylaxis, post-arrest, refractory shock |
| Dopamine | β1 → α (dose-dep) | Inotropy + chronotropy → vasoconstriction high-dose | Less favored — arrhythmia risk |
| Phenylephrine | α | Pure vasoconstriction | Anesthesia hypotension; selected; reflex bradycardia |
| Dobutamine | β1 > β2 | **Inotropy + mild vasodilation** | Cardiogenic shock; **may drop BP** |
| Milrinone | PDE-3 inhibitor | Inotrope + vasodilator | Cardiogenic with PHTN; longer t½ |
| Isoproterenol | β1 + β2 | Inotrope + vasodilator + chronotrope | Bradycardia bridge to pacing |

**Anaphylaxis:**

| Step | Drug |
|------|------|
| 1 | **IM epinephrine 0.3-0.5 mg lateral thigh** (don't delay for IV); repeat q5-15 min |
| 2 | Airway, oxygen, IV fluids |
| 3 | Antihistamine (H1: diphenhydramine; H2: ranitidine), nebulized albuterol for bronchospasm |
| 4 | Glucocorticoid (limited acute benefit; may help biphasic) |
| 5 | Observation 4-12h (biphasic risk) |
| 6 | Discharge with **2 epinephrine autoinjectors**, allergist follow-up |

**Cardiogenic shock specifics:**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Cause | Most often MI — STEMI gets emergent revascularization (PCI) |
| Initial | Vasopressor (norepi) + inotrope (dobutamine) |
| Mechanical support | IABP, Impella, VA-ECMO if refractory |
| Diuresis | Carefully when volume overload + perfusion adequate |
| Avoid β-blockers, large fluid boluses |

**Obstructive shock:**

| Cause | Treatment |
|-------|-----------|
| Tension pneumothorax | **Needle decompression** (2nd intercostal, midclavicular line, or 5th lateral) → chest tube |
| Cardiac tamponade | **Pericardiocentesis** |
| Massive PE | **Thrombolysis (tPA)**, embolectomy, ECMO |

**Hypovolemic / hemorrhagic shock:**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Stop bleeding | Pressure, tourniquet, surgery |
| Crystalloid | Limited; avoid over-resuscitation |
| Blood products | **Massive transfusion 1:1:1** (RBC : FFP : platelets); **TXA** within 3h of trauma |
| Goal | SBP ~90 (permissive hypotension until bleeding controlled, except TBI) |

**Adrenal crisis:**

| Trigger | Stress in chronic AI |
| Treatment | **Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV** + IVF + glucose + treat trigger |

**Common gotchas:**
- **Septic shock with persistently low MAP despite norepi** → add vasopressin, then hydrocortisone
- **Cardiogenic shock**: dobutamine drops BP — pair with norepi
- **Tension pneumothorax** is a clinical diagnosis — don't wait for CXR
- **β-blockers in pheo / cocaine HTN** → unopposed α — phentolamine first
- **Anaphylaxis** — give IM epi early; don't replace with antihistamine alone
- **Lactate elevation** can be from B1 deficiency, metformin, malignancy — not always perfusion
- **Permissive hypotension** in trauma to avoid clot disruption (target SBP ~90 until controlled)

**Rule of thumb:** Identify shock type → matching resuscitation. Distributive = norepi + fluids. Cardiogenic = norepi + dobutamine + revascularize. Obstructive = unblock the obstruction. Hypovolemic = stop bleeding + 1:1:1 + TXA. Anaphylaxis = IM epi.
