### Electrolyte Disorders

**Sodium — orient by **volume status** + **osm**:**

| Hyponatremia | Volume | Cause |
|--------------|--------|-------|
| Hypovolemic | ↓ | Diuretics, vomiting, diarrhea, addisonian, salt-wasting nephropathy |
| Euvolemic | Normal | **SIADH**, hypothyroid, glucocorticoid def, primary polydipsia, beer potomania, "tea and toast" |
| Hypervolemic | ↑ | HF, cirrhosis, nephrotic, CKD |

**Pseudohyponatremia** (normal osm) — hyperlipidemia, hyperproteinemia.
**Hypertonic hyponatremia** — hyperglycemia (correct ~1.6 mEq Na ↓ per 100 mg/dL glucose ↑), mannitol.

**SIADH labs (cardinal):**
- Low serum osm (<275)
- High urine osm (>100, often >300)
- Urine Na >40
- Euvolemic
- Normal thyroid, adrenal, renal function

**Hyponatremia treatment — by severity:**

| Severity | Treatment |
|----------|-----------|
| Severe symptoms (sz, coma) | **3% saline 100 mL bolus**, repeat × 2 if needed; raise Na 4-6 mEq/L |
| Moderate symptoms | 3% saline slower |
| Asymptomatic / chronic | Treat cause, fluid restriction, salt + protein, vaptans (selected) |

**Correction limits:**
- ≤8 mEq/L per 24h (avoid osmotic demyelination)
- ≤10-12 first 24h, ≤18 first 48h

**Hypernatremia:**

| Cause | Mechanism |
|-------|-----------|
| Inadequate water intake | Elderly, restricted access |
| Diabetes insipidus (central or nephrogenic) | ADH deficient or unresponsive |
| Osmotic diuresis | DKA, mannitol, post-AKI |
| GI losses | Diarrhea, NGT |

DI workup: water deprivation test → desmopressin response: central (responds), nephrogenic (no response).

**Free water deficit = TBW × ((Na/140) − 1)**. Replace 50% in 24h, then remainder. Don't drop Na faster than 10 mEq/L/day.

**Potassium:**

| Hypokalemia | Cause |
|-------------|-------|
| **GI losses** | Diarrhea, NGT |
| **Renal losses** | Diuretics, hyperaldo (Conn), Bartter, Gitelman, RTA type 1/2, hypomagnesemia |
| **Shift in** | Insulin, β-agonists, alkalosis, refeeding |

Replace **Mg first** if low — K won't rise until Mg corrected. EKG: U waves, flattened T, ST depression.

| Hyperkalemia | Cause |
|--------------|-------|
| AKI / CKD | ↓ excretion |
| Drugs | ACEi/ARB, MRA, NSAIDs, β-blocker, TMP, heparin |
| Cell shift | Acidosis (each 0.1 ↓ pH → ~0.6 ↑ K), tumor lysis, rhabdo, succinylcholine, insulin def |
| Adrenal insufficiency, type IV RTA | ↓ aldosterone |

**Hyperkalemia treatment ladder:**

| Severity | Action |
|----------|--------|
| EKG changes (peaked T, wide QRS, sine wave) | **Calcium gluconate** first |
| Move K into cells | Insulin + glucose, β2-agonist (albuterol), bicarb if acidotic |
| Remove K | Loop diuretic, K-binders (patiromer, sodium zirconium), dialysis if refractory |

**Calcium:**

| Hypocalcemia | Cause |
|--------------|-------|
| Vitamin D def, hypoparathyroid (post-thyroidectomy), CKD, hypomag, pancreatitis, transfusion (citrate), tumor lysis, sepsis |

**Pseudohypocalcemia from low albumin** — corrected Ca = Ca + 0.8 × (4 − albumin). Better: **ionized Ca**.

Symptoms: **Chvostek** (facial twitch), **Trousseau** (carpopedal spasm with cuff), tetany, perioral numbness, prolonged QT, seizures.

**Treatment:** symptomatic → IV calcium gluconate; chronic → oral Ca + vitamin D.

| Hypercalcemia | Cause |
|---------------|-------|
| **CHIMPANZEES**: Calcium supplement, Hyperparathyroid, Iatrogenic, Multiple myeloma/Mets/Milk-alkali, Paget disease, Addison, Neoplasm (PTHrP), Zollinger-Ellison, Excess vitamin A/D, Sarcoid (granulomatous) |

Top 2 causes: **primary hyperparathyroidism** (outpatient) + **malignancy** (inpatient — PTHrP from squamous, lytic mets, MM).

**Hypercalcemia treatment:**

| Severity | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Mild (≤12) asymptomatic | Treat cause |
| Moderate (12-14) symptomatic / severe (>14) | **IV NS** 200-300 mL/h (avoid Lasix unless overload), **calcitonin** (rapid, 24-48h tachyphylaxis), **bisphosphonate** (zoledronate — slow but lasting) |
| Refractory / malignancy | Denosumab (bisphos failure); steroids (granulomatous, lymphoma) |

**Magnesium:**

| Hypomagnesemia | Cause |
|----------------|-------|
| GI losses, diuretics, alcoholism, PPI long-term, refeeding, aminoglycosides, cisplatin |

Effect: refractory hypoK + hypoCa, **torsades de pointes**, weakness, tetany.

| Hypermagnesemia | Cause |
|-----------------|-------|
| Renal failure, antacids/laxatives (Mg-containing), eclampsia treatment, tumor lysis |

Symptoms: ↓ DTRs (early), respiratory depression, bradycardia, hypotension.

Treatment: stop intake, **calcium gluconate** for severe.

**Phosphate (briefly):**

| Disorder | Causes |
|----------|--------|
| ↓ PO4 | Refeeding, alcohol, DKA recovery, hyperparathyroid, vitamin D def |
| ↑ PO4 | CKD, tumor lysis, rhabdo, hypoparathyroid |

**Common gotchas:**
- Always check **Mg** in refractory hypoK, hypoCa
- **Glucose correction**: every 100 ↑ glucose → ~1.6 ↓ Na (correct *up*, real Na may be normal)
- **Pseudohyperkalemia** — hemolyzed sample, fist-pumping during draw, very high WBC/platelets
- **3% saline** for severe symptomatic hyponatremia — don't wait
- **Calcium gluconate** in hyperK is for **cardiac membrane stabilization** — doesn't lower K but buys time
- **Bicarb** doesn't help hyperK acutely unless acidotic; effect is delayed
- **Albumin-corrected Ca** still imperfect — get ionized

**Rule of thumb:** Sodium = water problem (volume status + osm). K disorders are urgent if EKG-positive (Ca first). Mg precedes Ca and K corrections. Hypercalcemia: hydrate, calcitonin, bisphosphonate.
