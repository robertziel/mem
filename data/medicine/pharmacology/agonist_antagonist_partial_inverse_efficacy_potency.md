### Agonists, Antagonists, Efficacy, Potency

**Drug-receptor classification:**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Full agonist | Binds + maximally activates | Morphine, isoproterenol, ACh |
| Partial agonist | Binds + sub-max activation (low intrinsic activity) | Buprenorphine, pindolol, varenicline, aripiprazole |
| Inverse agonist | Binds + opposite of agonist (negative intrinsic activity) | β-carbolines (GABA-A) |
| Competitive antagonist | Binds active site, no activation, **surmountable** by ↑ agonist | Naloxone, prazosin, propranolol |
| Non-competitive antagonist | Binds elsewhere or irreversibly, **NOT surmountable** | Aspirin on COX, phenoxybenzamine |
| Functional antagonist | Opposite physiologic effect via different receptor | Epinephrine vs histamine in anaphylaxis |
| Chemical antagonist | Inactivates the agonist | Protamine vs heparin |

**Efficacy vs potency:**

| | Definition | Curve effect |
|---|-----------|--------------|
| **Potency** | Dose required for effect (EC50) | Left shift = more potent |
| **Efficacy** | Maximum response (Emax) | Higher plateau = more effective |

**More potent ≠ better drug.** Higher efficacy is the clinically meaningful goal; potency just tells you the dose.

**Effects on dose-response curves:**

| Modifier | Emax | EC50 | Curve shape |
|----------|------|------|-------------|
| Competitive antagonist | Unchanged | ↑ (right shift) | Same shape, shifted |
| Non-competitive antagonist | ↓ | ~ | Lowered plateau |
| Partial agonist (alone) | ↓ | ~ | Lowered plateau |
| Partial agonist + full agonist | ↓ | ↑ | Like a competitive antagonist (it occupies receptor without full activation) |

**Why partial agonists matter clinically:**

| Drug | Use case |
|------|----------|
| Buprenorphine | Opioid use disorder — ceiling effect limits respiratory depression |
| Varenicline | Smoking cessation — partial nicotinic agonist; reduces craving + blocks reward |
| Pindolol | β-blocker with partial agonism — less bradycardia |
| Aripiprazole | Atypical antipsychotic — partial D2 agonist (stabilizer) |

**Quantitative concepts:**

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Affinity (Kd) | [drug] at which 50% of receptors are occupied |
| EC50 | [drug] at which 50% of effect is observed |
| Emax | Maximum biological response |
| LD50 | Dose lethal in 50% |
| ED50 | Effective dose in 50% |
| Therapeutic index (TI) | LD50 / ED50 |
| Therapeutic window | Range between minimum effective and toxic concentrations |

**Drugs with narrow therapeutic index** (need monitoring):

| Drug | Toxicity |
|------|----------|
| Warfarin | INR — bleeding |
| Digoxin | Visual halos, arrhythmia, GI |
| Lithium | Tremor, ataxia, seizures, renal |
| Phenytoin | Nystagmus, ataxia |
| Theophylline | Seizures, arrhythmia |
| Aminoglycosides | Nephro/oto-toxicity |
| Cyclosporine, tacrolimus | Nephrotoxicity |
| Methotrexate | Marrow, mucositis |

**Tachyphylaxis vs tolerance:**

| Term | Time | Examples |
|------|------|---------|
| Tachyphylaxis | Rapid (minutes-hours) | Nitroglycerin, β-agonists, MDMA |
| Tolerance | Days-weeks | Opioids, benzos, alcohol |

**Common gotchas:**
- A **competitive antagonist** can be overcome by more agonist; **non-competitive** cannot — clinically matters in overdose reversal
- **Naloxone** competes with opioid; need enough to outcompete (especially fentanyl), and short t½ → repeat dosing
- **Partial agonist** can act as antagonist when a full agonist is present (occupies the receptor without full activation)
- **Aspirin** is a non-competitive (irreversible) antagonist — effect persists for platelet lifetime (~7-10 days)
- High potency without high efficacy is useless

**Rule of thumb:** Potency = where on the X-axis (dose); efficacy = where on the Y-axis (max effect). Surmountable shift = competitive; lowered plateau = non-competitive or partial agonist.
