### Substance Use — Intoxication & Withdrawal

**Major substances:**

| Class | Examples |
|-------|----------|
| Depressants | Alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, GHB |
| Opioids | Heroin, fentanyl, oxycodone, methadone, buprenorphine |
| Stimulants | Cocaine, amphetamines, methamphetamine, MDMA |
| Hallucinogens | LSD, psilocybin, mescaline, PCP, ketamine |
| Cannabinoids | THC, synthetic |
| Nicotine | Cigarettes, vape |
| Inhalants | Toluene, NO2 |

**Intoxication vs withdrawal — opposites:**

| Drug | Intoxication | Withdrawal |
|------|--------------|------------|
| Alcohol | Disinhibition, slurred speech, ataxia, nystagmus, sedation; severe → coma, respiratory depression | Tremor (6-24h), hallucinations (12-48h), seizures (24-48h), **DT (48-96h)** |
| Opioids | **Pinpoint pupils**, ↓RR, ↓consciousness, hypotension, ↓bowel sounds | Lacrimation, rhinorrhea, yawning, **piloerection**, dilated pupils, diarrhea, cramps, anxiety; **uncomfortable, not life-threatening** |
| Benzodiazepines | Sedation, ataxia, slurred speech (similar to alcohol but minimal RR depression alone; lethal with opioid) | Tremor, anxiety, **seizures, delirium** (life-threatening) |
| Cocaine / amphetamines | Mydriasis, tachycardia, HTN, hyperthermia, agitation, paranoia, chest pain, MI | Crash: dysphoria, fatigue, ↑sleep, ↑appetite |
| LSD / hallucinogens | Visual hallucinations, dilated pupils, ↑HR/BP, synesthesia | None typically |
| PCP | **Vertical & horizontal nystagmus**, violence, dissociation, analgesia | None; flashbacks |
| MDMA | Empathogenic, hyperthermia, hyponatremia, jaw clenching, serotonin syndrome | Mood crash |
| Cannabis | Conjunctival injection, ↑appetite, anxiety/paranoia at high dose | Mild — irritability, sleep, appetite ↓ |
| Nicotine | Stimulation, ↑HR/BP | Craving, irritability, ↑appetite, weight gain |

**Alcohol withdrawal timeline:**

| Time | Stage |
|------|-------|
| 6-24h | Tremor, anxiety, autonomic |
| 12-48h | **Alcoholic hallucinations** (still oriented) |
| 24-48h | **Withdrawal seizures** |
| 48-96h | **Delirium tremens** — fever, tachycardia, HTN, confusion, hallucinations, **mortality 5%** |

CIWA-Ar score guides treatment.

**Alcohol withdrawal treatment:**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Benzodiazepines** | First-line; lorazepam (LFT issues), diazepam (long-acting); CIWA-driven dosing |
| **Thiamine before glucose** | Prevent Wernicke |
| Fluids, electrolytes (K, Mg, PO4) | — |
| Monitor | Vital signs, mental status, seizures |
| Adjunct | Phenobarbital for severe / benzo-resistant; gabapentin for mild |
| Anti-epileptic | Not routine; treat status if it happens |

**Wernicke-Korsakoff:**

| Wernicke | Korsakoff |
|----------|-----------|
| Acute, reversible | Chronic, often permanent |
| **COA**: Confusion, Ophthalmoplegia, Ataxia | Anterograde amnesia, **confabulation** |
| Treat: thiamine **before glucose**; high-dose IV thiamine for several days | — |

**Opioid intoxication:**

| Sign | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Triad**: ↓RR, ↓consciousness, **pinpoint pupils** | Classic |
| Treatment | **Naloxone IV/IM/IN** (0.04-0.4 mg, may repeat / drip; higher for fentanyl); ventilatory support |

**Opioid use disorder treatment (MAT):**

| Drug | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| **Methadone** | Full agonist; OTP only in US |
| **Buprenorphine** (or buprenorphine-naloxone) | Partial agonist; ceiling effect ↓ overdose |
| **Naltrexone (extended-release)** | Antagonist; needs 7-10d opioid-free first |

Combine with counseling / behavioral support.

**Opioid withdrawal management (symptomatic):**

| Symptom | Drug |
|---------|------|
| Autonomic | Clonidine, lofexidine |
| Diarrhea | Loperamide |
| Nausea | Ondansetron |
| Insomnia / anxiety | Trazodone, hydroxyzine |
| Cravings | Buprenorphine, methadone |

**Stimulant intoxication / overdose:**

| Issue | Action |
|-------|--------|
| Agitation | **Benzodiazepines first-line** |
| Hypertension / chest pain | Avoid β-blocker monotherapy (unopposed α — esp. with cocaine); use phentolamine, CCB, nitrates; benzo first |
| Hyperthermia | Cooling, IV fluids |
| MI | Nitrates, benzo, then ACS protocol; **avoid β-blocker alone** |
| Seizures | Benzodiazepines |

**Cocaine MI** has same urgency as ACS; PCI may be needed.

**Benzodiazepine overdose:**

| Action |
|--------|
| Supportive (airway, IVF) |
| **Flumazenil** rarely — risk of withdrawal seizures, especially in chronic users |

**Acetaminophen toxicity (frequent intentional OD):**

| Phase | Time | Findings |
|-------|------|----------|
| 1 | 0-24h | Asymptomatic / nausea |
| 2 | 24-72h | RUQ pain, ↑AST/ALT |
| 3 | 72-96h | Fulminant hepatic failure |
| 4 | 4-14d | Recovery or death |

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| Level | At 4h post-ingestion; plot on **Rumack-Matthew nomogram** |
| Treat | **N-acetylcysteine** (within 8h ideal; benefit even later) |

**Common gotchas:**
- **Don't β-block cocaine** alone — phentolamine + benzo
- **Wernicke**: thiamine **before** glucose
- **Naltrexone** requires 7-10d opioid-free or precipitated withdrawal
- **Buprenorphine starts** require mild withdrawal (COWS ≥8) to avoid precipitated withdrawal
- **Flumazenil** can precipitate seizures
- **Methadone** prolongs QT; check ECG with high doses
- **Body packers** with cocaine — surgical removal if rupture; whole-bowel irrigation otherwise

**Rule of thumb:** Pinpoint + ↓RR + ↓LOC = opioid → naloxone. Withdrawal seizures with alcohol/benzo (life-threatening) but not opioid (uncomfortable, not lethal). Wernicke → thiamine first. Cocaine intox → benzo, no β-blocker alone. APAP → NAC.
