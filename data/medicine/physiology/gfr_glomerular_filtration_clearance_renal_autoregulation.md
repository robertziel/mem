### GFR, Clearance, Renal Autoregulation

**Definitions at a glance:**

| Term | Formula / meaning | Normal |
|------|-------------------|--------|
| GFR | Volume filtered/min across all glomeruli | ~120 mL/min/1.73m² |
| Clearance | (U×V) / P — volume of plasma cleared per unit time | Substance-specific |
| RPF (renal plasma flow) | PAH clearance (filtered + secreted, ~100% extracted) | ~600 mL/min |
| RBF | RPF / (1 − Hct) | ~1100 mL/min (~25% CO) |
| Filtration fraction | GFR / RPF | ~20% |

**Markers used clinically:**

| Marker | Use | Caveats |
|--------|-----|---------|
| Inulin | Gold standard GFR (freely filtered, not reabsorbed/secreted) | Research only |
| Creatinine clearance | Estimate GFR | Slightly overestimates (tubular secretion) |
| Cystatin C | More accurate in extremes of muscle mass | Less available |
| BUN | Volume status, not GFR alone | ↑ in dehydration, GI bleed, steroids |
| eGFR (CKD-EPI) | Routine clinical estimate | Validated in steady state only |

**Starling forces at glomerulus:**

| Force | Direction | Driver |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Glomerular capillary hydrostatic (PGC) | Out | Promotes filtration |
| Bowman's capsule hydrostatic (PBC) | In | Opposes (↑ in obstruction) |
| Glomerular oncotic (πGC) | In | Plasma proteins |
| Bowman's oncotic (πBC) | ~0 | Normally negligible |

GFR ∝ PGC − PBC − πGC

**Afferent vs efferent arteriole effects:**

| Action | RPF | GFR | FF |
|--------|-----|-----|-----|
| Constrict afferent | ↓ | ↓ | ~ |
| Dilate afferent (NSAIDs lost!) | ↑ | ↑ | ~ |
| Constrict efferent (ATII; ACEi/ARB lost!) | ↓ | ↑ | ↑↑ |
| Dilate efferent | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ |

**Drug pearls:**
- **NSAIDs** block prostaglandin → afferent constriction → ↓GFR (especially if volume-depleted)
- **ACEi/ARB** block AT II → efferent dilation → ↓GFR (acceptable up to 30% rise in Cr; bigger jump = bilateral RAS)

**Renal autoregulation:**

| Mechanism | Trigger | Effect |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| Myogenic | ↑ stretch in afferent | Smooth muscle constricts |
| Tubuloglomerular feedback | ↑ NaCl at macula densa → adenosine | Afferent constricts |

Maintains GFR over MAP 80-180 mmHg; below 80 → GFR drops linearly.

**Common gotchas:**
- Cr lags AKI by 24-48h; don't trust a single value early
- Muscle wasting hides high Cr → eGFR falsely reassuring; check Cystatin C
- "Pre-renal" with FENa <1% only valid if not on diuretics; use FEUrea instead
- BUN:Cr >20:1 → pre-renal (or GI bleed); <10:1 → intrinsic or low-protein

**Rule of thumb:** GFR is the kidney's vital sign; afferent dilation and efferent constriction both protect GFR — losing either drug class can drop it acutely.
