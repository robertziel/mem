### DKA vs HHS

**Side-by-side:**

| Feature | DKA | HHS |
|---------|-----|-----|
| Population | T1 mostly; T2 sometimes | T2 (older, dehydrated) |
| Glucose | Often 250-600 (can be euglycemic on SGLT2i) | **>600**, often >1000 |
| pH | <7.30 | >7.30 |
| Bicarb | <18 | >18 |
| Anion gap | **>10-12 (high)** | Normal |
| Ketones | **Positive** (β-hydroxybutyrate) | Minimal |
| Serum osm | Variable | **>320 mOsm/kg** |
| Mental status | Variable | **Often altered, coma** |
| Volume deficit | 5-7 L | **8-10 L** |
| Onset | Hours | Days-weeks |
| Mortality | <5% | **5-20%** |

**Triggers (5 I's):** Infection, Infarction (MI, stroke), Insulin missed, Indiscretion (alcohol, cocaine), Iatrogenic (steroids, antipsychotics, SGLT2i for euglycemic DKA).

**DKA diagnostic triad:**
1. Hyperglycemia (or "euglycemic DKA")
2. Acidosis (pH <7.30 or HCO3 <18)
3. Ketones (β-hydroxybutyrate)

**DKA management algorithm:**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| 1. **Fluids** | Isotonic NS first hour (1-1.5 L); then 0.45% NS or NS based on corrected Na. **Switch to D5 + 0.45% NS when glucose <200** |
| 2. **Potassium** | Check K **before** insulin: K <3.3 → hold insulin, replete K to 3.3 first; K 3.3-5.2 → replete + start insulin; K >5.2 → start insulin without K |
| 3. **Insulin** | Regular insulin **0.1 U/kg/h IV drip** (some skip bolus). When glucose <200: continue drip + add D5 to fluids; aim AG closure |
| 4. **Bicarbonate** | **Only if pH <6.9** — controversial otherwise |
| 5. **Phosphate** | Replete only if severe / cardiac dysfunction; routine repletion not recommended |

**Transition to subcutaneous insulin:**
- **Anion gap closed**, HCO3 ≥18, pH ≥7.30, patient eating
- Overlap SC + IV by 1-2h to prevent rebound

**HHS management:**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| 1. **Aggressive fluids** | Volume deficit larger; 1-1.5 L NS first hour; total often 8-10 L over 12-24h |
| 2. Insulin | Lower-dose insulin drip (0.05 U/kg/h or even 0.025); hyperglycemia corrects partly with fluid alone |
| 3. Electrolytes | K, PO4, Mg often deficient; correct |
| 4. Trigger | Search aggressively (infection, MI, stroke) |
| 5. Mental status | Improves slowly with osm correction; **don't drop osm too fast** (cerebral edema risk) |

**Cerebral edema risk** — children with DKA, very rapid fluid / glucose drop. Watch for headache, lethargy, hypertension, bradycardia → mannitol or hypertonic saline.

**Anion gap math:**

| Formula |
|---------|
| AG = Na − (Cl + HCO3); normal 8-12 |
| Corrected glucose Na: actual Na + 1.6 × ((glucose − 100)/100) |
| Effective osm: 2(Na) + glucose/18 |

**Why euglycemic DKA happens (SGLT2i):**
- Drug excretes glucose → glucose normal
- But ↑ glucagon, ↓ insulin, ↑ ketogenesis still occur
- Patient looks "okay glucose" but has ketoacidosis
- **Hold SGLT2i for sick days, surgery, fasting** (3-4 days perioperatively)

**Common mistakes:**

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---------|-------------|
| Insulin before K check / repletion | Severe hypokalemia, arrhythmia |
| Stop insulin when glucose normal but AG still open | Recurrent DKA |
| Bicarb routinely | No benefit, possibly harm |
| Too rapid osm correction in HHS | Cerebral edema |
| Missing trigger | Patient returns / dies of underlying problem |
| Stopping subQ insulin in T1 sick day | Triggered DKA |

**Common gotchas:**
- **Mucormycosis** in DKA — facial pain, black eschar, sinus involvement; emergent surgery + amphotericin
- **β-hydroxybutyrate** is the dominant ketone in DKA but the **nitroprusside test** detects acetoacetate — false-negative early. Order direct β-OH-butyrate.
- "Pseudohyponatremia" with very high glucose — correct Na before treating
- HHS has higher mortality than DKA — older + bigger fluid deficit + comorbidities
- DKA precipitated by **MI** in T2 → check troponin, ECG

**Rule of thumb:** DKA = ketosis + acidosis + AG. HHS = osmolar crisis without ketones. Treat both with fluids + insulin + K + trigger search. AG closure (not glucose) ends the DKA. Older T2 + altered MS + glucose >600 → HHS.
