### Thyroid Disorders

**Hypothyroidism — etiology:**

| Cause | Clue |
|-------|------|
| **Hashimoto thyroiditis** | Most common in iodine-replete; anti-TPO, anti-TG; goiter ± atrophy |
| Iodine deficiency | Endemic regions |
| Post-thyroidectomy / RAI | History |
| Drug-induced | Lithium, amiodarone (also hyper), checkpoint inhibitors |
| Central (rare) | Pituitary disease — TSH inappropriately normal/low with low T4 |
| Subacute (de Quervain) | Post-viral, painful, transient — can be hyper then hypo |
| Postpartum thyroiditis | Within 1y postpartum, often transient |

**Hyperthyroidism — etiology:**

| Cause | Clue |
|-------|------|
| **Graves** | Most common; diffuse goiter, **TSI / TRAb**, exophthalmos, pretibial myxedema |
| Toxic multinodular goiter | Older patient; nodular gland |
| Toxic adenoma | Single hot nodule |
| Subacute thyroiditis | Painful, transient hyperthyroid → hypo → euthyroid |
| Silent / postpartum | Painless |
| Factitious | Surreptitious thyroid hormone |
| TSH-secreting adenoma | Rare |
| Iodine-induced (Jod-Basedow) | Contrast, amiodarone in autonomous gland |

**Lab patterns:**

| Pattern | TSH | Free T4 | Diagnosis |
|---------|-----|---------|-----------|
| Primary hypothyroid | ↑ | ↓ | Hashimoto, post-Tx |
| Subclinical hypothyroid | ↑ | normal | Many |
| Central hypothyroid | normal/↓ | ↓ | Pituitary |
| Primary hyperthyroid | ↓ | ↑ | Graves, TMG, etc. |
| Subclinical hyperthyroid | ↓ | normal | Often early |
| Sick euthyroid | ↓ then ↑ recovery | low T3, normal T4 | Hospitalized non-thyroidal illness |

**Hypothyroidism treatment:**

| Detail |
|--------|
| **Levothyroxine** 1.6 mcg/kg/day; lower in elderly / cardiac (start 25-50 mcg) |
| Take **on empty stomach**, separate from calcium, iron, PPI |
| Recheck TSH at 6 weeks after change |
| Pregnancy: ↑ dose ~30% immediately on positive test; target TSH lower (trimester-specific) |
| Myxedema coma: IV levothyroxine + hydrocortisone (cover possible adrenal insufficiency) + supportive |

**Hyperthyroidism treatment:**

| Modality | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Methimazole** | First-line for Graves (NOT in 1st trimester — hepatotoxic, agranulocytosis) |
| **Propylthiouracil (PTU)** | 1st trimester pregnancy, thyroid storm; hepatotoxicity, ANCA vasculitis |
| Radioactive iodine (I-131) | Definitive in non-pregnant, non-lactating; orbitopathy can flare (steroid prep) |
| Surgery (thyroidectomy) | Large goiter, severe orbitopathy, pregnancy intolerant of meds |
| **β-blocker** | Symptomatic relief — propranolol, atenolol |

**Pregnancy management:**
- 1st trimester: **PTU** (lower teratogen risk)
- 2nd-3rd trimester: switch to **methimazole** (lower hepatotoxic risk)
- Goal: TSH normal range, FT4 upper-normal

**Thyroid storm:**

| Burch-Wartofsky points (selected) |
|-----------------------------------|
| Temperature, CNS effects, GI/hepatic, CV, precipitating event |

Treatment ladder:
1. **β-blocker** (propranolol IV — controls HR + ↓ T4→T3)
2. **PTU** (preferred over methimazole here — also blocks T4→T3)
3. **Iodine ≥1h after PTU** (Wolff-Chaikoff via Lugol or potassium iodide; **never before** PTU — would feed substrate)
4. **Hydrocortisone** (↓ peripheral conversion + cover adrenal)
5. Cool, fluid, treat trigger
6. Cholestyramine (binds enterohepatic recirculation)

**Thyroid nodules workup:**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| 1 | TSH first |
| 2a (low TSH) | Radioactive iodine scan — hot nodule rarely cancer; treat as hyperthyroid |
| 2b (normal/high TSH) | Ultrasound — TI-RADS / Bethesda |
| 3 | FNA biopsy by size + features (≥1 cm with suspicious features, ≥1.5-2 cm without) |

**Bethesda classification (FNA):**

| Bethesda | Result | Cancer risk |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| I | Non-diagnostic | Repeat |
| II | Benign | <3% |
| III | AUS / FLUS | 5-15% |
| IV | Follicular neoplasm | 15-30% |
| V | Suspicious for malignancy | 60-75% |
| VI | Malignant | >97% |

**Thyroid cancers:**

| Type | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Papillary (most) | Lymphatic spread, "Orphan Annie" nuclei, psammoma bodies; good prognosis |
| Follicular | Hematogenous, capsular invasion on histology |
| Medullary | C-cell origin (calcitonin); MEN2 association — check **RET**, calcitonin, CEA |
| Anaplastic | Elderly, poor prognosis, rapid growth |
| Lymphoma | Often arises in Hashimoto |

**Common gotchas:**
- **Subclinical hypothyroidism** treat if TSH >10, anti-TPO+, pregnancy, symptomatic
- **Amiodarone-induced thyrotoxicosis** — type 1 (iodine-induced in autonomous gland) vs type 2 (destructive) — treat differently (methimazole vs steroids)
- **Hashitoxicosis** — early Hashimoto release of stored hormone; transient hyper before chronic hypo
- **Don't give iodine before PTU/methimazole** in storm
- **Pregnancy hCG-mediated mild hyperthyroidism** (gestational transient thyrotoxicosis) — usually resolves; don't treat unless symptomatic / persistent
- **Subacute thyroiditis (de Quervain)** — painful neck, post-viral, **NSAIDs / steroids**, no antithyroid drugs

**Rule of thumb:** TSH first. Hypo → levothyroxine on empty stomach. Hyper → β-blocker + methimazole (PTU 1st trim or storm). Storm: β-blocker → PTU → iodine ≥1h later → steroid. Nodules: TSH → US → FNA per criteria.
