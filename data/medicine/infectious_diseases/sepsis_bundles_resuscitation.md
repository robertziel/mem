### Sepsis & Septic Shock

**Definitions (Sepsis-3):**

| Term | Criteria |
|------|----------|
| Sepsis | Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysregulated host response to infection — operationalized as **infection + ≥2 SOFA points** |
| Septic shock | Sepsis + persisting hypotension requiring vasopressor to maintain MAP ≥65 + lactate >2 despite adequate fluid resuscitation |
| qSOFA (bedside, deprecated for diagnosis but still used for screening) | RR ≥22, altered mental status, SBP ≤100 (≥2 = high risk) |

**SIRS criteria (older):** T >38 / <36, HR >90, RR >20, WBC >12 / <4. Less specific.

**Hour-1 bundle (Surviving Sepsis 2021):**

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| 1. **Lactate** | Measure; remeasure at 2h if elevated |
| 2. **Blood cultures × 2** | Before antibiotics if no significant delay |
| 3. **Broad-spectrum antibiotics** | Within **1h** of recognition (immune-impaired) or **3h** (low-probability sepsis); empirical |
| 4. **30 mL/kg crystalloid** for hypotension or lactate ≥4 | Within 3h |
| 5. **Vasopressors** for MAP ≥65 if not responding to fluids | Add early |

**Empirical antibiotic choice (broad-spectrum, narrow once data return):**

| Coverage | Drugs |
|----------|-------|
| Gram-positive (incl. MRSA) | Vancomycin or linezolid |
| Gram-negative (incl. Pseudomonas) | Pip-tazo, cefepime, meropenem |
| Atypical (if pneumonia source) | Add azithromycin or doxycycline |
| Anaerobic (intra-abdominal, aspiration) | Pip-tazo, meropenem, metronidazole add-on |
| Suspected source-specific (CNS, CAP, intra-abdominal, urosepsis) | Tailor |

**Source control:**

| Source | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Abscess | Drainage |
| Cholangitis | ERCP |
| Necrotizing fasciitis | Surgical debridement |
| Infected catheter | Remove |
| Toxic megacolon, perforation | Surgery |
| Pyelonephritis with obstruction | Stent / drainage |

**Vasopressors:**

| Drug | First-line / Note |
|------|-------------------|
| **Norepinephrine** | First-line in septic shock |
| Vasopressin | Add if escalating norepi (~0.25-0.5 mcg/kg/min) |
| Epinephrine | Refractory |
| Dobutamine | Cardiac dysfunction with low cardiac output |
| Phenylephrine | Pure α — limited niche |
| Hydrocortisone | If on multiple vasopressors / refractory shock |

**Fluid choice:**

| Fluid | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Balanced crystalloid (LR, Plasma-Lyte)** | Preferred over normal saline — less hyperchloremic acidosis, AKI signal |
| Normal saline | Acceptable; cheap |
| Albumin | Adjunct in severe sepsis with high-volume requirements; SBP |
| **Avoid** synthetic colloids (HES — kidney injury) | — |

**Lactate clearance:** Reduction over 2-6h is the resuscitation marker. Persistent elevation despite normalized vitals → ongoing inadequate perfusion (or non-perfusion cause: liver failure, B1 deficiency, cyanide, metformin).

**Common infection sources to image:**

| Source | Imaging / test |
|--------|----------------|
| Pulmonary | CXR / CT, sputum |
| Urinary | UA, urine culture, renal US if obstruction |
| Intra-abdominal | CT abdomen/pelvis, surgical |
| Skin / soft tissue | Exam, MRI for nec fasc |
| CNS | LP, CT/MRI |
| Endocarditis | Blood cultures × 3, TTE / TEE |
| Catheter | Remove + culture tip |

**Septic shock vs other shock states:**

| Type | CVP | Cardiac output | SVR | Wedge | Mixed-venous O2 |
|------|-----|---------------|-----|-------|-----------------|
| Septic (distributive) | Low / normal | **High** | **Low** | Low | Often **high** (decreased extraction) |
| Cardiogenic | High | **Low** | High | High | Low |
| Hypovolemic | Low | Low | High | Low | Low |
| Obstructive (PE, tamponade, tension PTX) | High (RV strain) | Low | High | Variable | Low |

**Steroids in septic shock:**

| Indication |
|-----------|
| Refractory shock requiring escalating vasopressors → hydrocortisone 200 mg/day |
| ARDS adjunct (selected) |

**Glucose control:** target 140-180 mg/dL.

**DVT prophylaxis, stress ulcer prophylaxis** if on mechanical ventilation or high bleed risk.

**Common gotchas:**
- **Antibiotics within 1h** of recognition saves lives — don't wait for cultures if obtaining is delayed
- **Lactate elevation can be non-perfusion**: thiamine deficiency, metformin lactic acidosis, severe liver failure, cyanide
- **30 mL/kg may be too much** in HFrEF / dialysis-dependent — guide further fluid by dynamic measures (passive leg raise, US, stroke volume variation)
- **Source control** is critical — antibiotics alone don't cure undrained abscess or necrotizing fasciitis
- **Procalcitonin** can guide stop / start of antibiotics
- **Toxic shock** — staphylococcal (TSST-1 superantigen), streptococcal — both severe; clindamycin **adds toxin suppression**

**Rule of thumb:** Suspect → cultures → antibiotics within 1h → 30 mL/kg crystalloid → vasopressor → norepi first → reassess lactate → source control. Add hydrocortisone if refractory. Balanced crystalloid > saline.
