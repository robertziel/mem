### Congenital Heart Defects

**Acyanotic vs cyanotic split:**

| Type | Shunt | Defects |
|------|-------|---------|
| Acyanotic (L→R) | Left-to-right (eventually reverses → Eisenmenger) | VSD, ASD, PDA, coarctation |
| Cyanotic (R→L) | Right-to-left from birth | 5 T's: TGA, ToF, Truncus, TAPVR, Tricuspid atresia |

**Acyanotic defects:**

| Lesion | Murmur / clue | Notes |
|--------|---------------|-------|
| VSD | Holosystolic, harsh, lower L sternal border | Most common congenital; small often closes |
| ASD | Wide fixed-split S2, mid-systolic ejection | Paradoxical embolus risk |
| PDA | Continuous "machine-like" murmur, L infraclavicular | Indomethacin closes; PGE keeps open (until repair) |
| Coarctation | Upper-extremity HTN, weak femoral pulses, rib notching (adult) | Associated with bicuspid AV, Turner |

**Cyanotic defects ("5 T's"):**

| Lesion | Mechanism | Clue |
|--------|-----------|------|
| **T**ransposition of great arteries | Aorta from RV, PA from LV → parallel circuits | Cyanosis at birth, needs PFO/PDA/VSD to mix; PGE life-saving |
| **T**etralogy of Fallot | RV outflow obstruction, VSD, overriding aorta, RVH | Boot-shaped heart, "Tet spells" relieved by squatting |
| Persistent **T**runcus arteriosus | Single great vessel | Failed AP septum (neural crest) |
| **T**ricuspid atresia | No tricuspid valve | Requires ASD + VSD; LV-dominant on EKG |
| **T**APVR (total anomalous pulm venous return) | Pulm veins → R atrium, not LA | "Snowman" silhouette (supracardiac type) |

Also cyanotic: Ebstein's anomaly (apically displaced TV, Li-exposure), HLHS.

**Tetralogy of Fallot — 4 features (PROVe):**
- **P**ulmonary stenosis (most important — drives severity)
- **R**VH
- **O**verriding aorta
- **V**SD
"Tet spells" = acute cyanosis with crying/feeding. Treat: knees-to-chest (↑ SVR pushes blood across PS into lungs), O2, morphine, β-blocker.

**Ductus arteriosus pharmacology (recognize at a glance):**

| Goal | Drug | Mechanism |
|------|------|-----------|
| Close PDA | Indomethacin / ibuprofen | Block PGE2 |
| Keep PDA open (ductal-dependent lesions) | PGE1 (alprostadil) | Maintain prostaglandin-driven patency |

**Ductal-dependent lesions** (PDA must stay open until surgery):
- TGA (mixing)
- HLHS, severe coarctation, interrupted arch (systemic flow)
- Pulmonary atresia, severe ToF (pulmonary flow)

**Maternal/syndrome associations:**

| Exposure / syndrome | Defect |
|---------------------|--------|
| Lithium | Ebstein |
| Alcohol | VSD, ASD, ToF |
| Rubella | PDA, pulm artery stenosis, cataracts |
| DM | TGA |
| Down syndrome | Endocardial cushion → AVSD, VSD |
| Turner | Coarctation, bicuspid AV |
| DiGeorge (22q11) | Truncus, ToF, interrupted aortic arch |
| Marfan | Aortic root dilation, MV prolapse |
| Williams | Supravalvular AS |

**Eisenmenger syndrome:** untreated L→R shunt → pulmonary HTN → reversal to R→L → late cyanosis, clubbing, polycythemia.

**Common gotchas:**
- "Cyanosis at birth" = TGA most likely (other 5T's may present hours-days later)
- Squatting helps Tet spells (↑ SVR, ↑ pulmonary flow); not other cyanotic lesions
- VSD murmur intensity is **inverse** to defect size (small = loud)
- Differential cyanosis (lower body cyanotic, upper pink) → coarctation with PDA

**Rule of thumb:** Cyanotic at birth → TGA; "boot heart" + Tet spells → ToF; machine murmur → PDA; rib notching + HTN → coarctation; ductal-dependent lesions need PGE until repair.
