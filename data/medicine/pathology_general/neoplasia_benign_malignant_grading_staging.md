### Neoplasia — Benign vs Malignant, Grading, Staging

**Benign vs malignant:**

| Feature | Benign | Malignant |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Differentiation | Well | Variable (well → anaplastic) |
| Growth rate | Slow | Fast |
| Local invasion | No (encapsulated) | Yes |
| Metastasis | **No** | Yes |
| Mitoses | Few, normal | Many, abnormal |
| Necrosis | Rare | Common |
| Effect on host | Pressure | Cachexia, mass effect, paraneoplastic |

**Naming conventions:**

| Tissue origin | Benign suffix | Malignant suffix |
|---------------|--------------|------------------|
| Epithelial | -oma (adenoma, papilloma) | **Carcinoma** (adenocarcinoma, SCC) |
| Mesenchymal | -oma (lipoma, leiomyoma) | **Sarcoma** (liposarcoma, leiomyosarcoma) |
| Blood / lymph | — | Leukemia, lymphoma |
| Germ cell | Teratoma | Seminoma, choriocarcinoma |
| Glial | (Various) | Glioma, glioblastoma |
| Melanocyte | Nevus | Melanoma |
| Misnomers (sound benign, are malignant) | — | **Hepatoma**, **lymphoma**, **melanoma**, **seminoma**, **mesothelioma**, **glioma** |

**Hallmarks of cancer (Hanahan-Weinberg):**

| Hallmark | Mechanism |
|----------|-----------|
| Self-sufficient growth signals | Oncogenes (RAS, MYC) |
| Insensitivity to anti-growth | Loss of tumor suppressors (Rb, p53) |
| Evasion of apoptosis | BCL2 ↑, p53 loss |
| Limitless replication | Telomerase reactivation |
| Sustained angiogenesis | VEGF |
| Tissue invasion / metastasis | E-cadherin loss, MMP |
| Immune evasion | PD-L1 |
| Genome instability | DNA repair defects |
| Reprogrammed metabolism | Warburg (glycolysis even with O2) |
| Tumor-promoting inflammation | Cytokines |

**Grading vs staging:**

| | Grading | Staging |
|---|---------|---------|
| Measures | Differentiation (microscopic) | Spread (clinical/pathologic) |
| Scale | G1-G4 (well → poorly differentiated) | TNM: Tumor size/extent, Nodes, Metastasis |
| Importance for prognosis | Lower | **Higher** (stage > grade) |

**TNM staging (general):**

| Letter | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| T | Primary tumor (size, invasion depth) |
| N | Regional lymph nodes (number, location) |
| M | Distant metastasis (any vs none) |

**Most common cancers (US):**

| Sex | Incidence | Mortality |
|-----|-----------|-----------|
| Male | Prostate > Lung > Colorectal | Lung > Prostate > Colorectal |
| Female | Breast > Lung > Colorectal | Lung > Breast > Colorectal |

**Common metastatic destinations:**

| Site | Most common metastases from |
|------|----------------------------|
| Brain | Lung > breast > melanoma > kidney > colon |
| Lung | Breast, colon, kidney, bladder, melanoma, sarcoma |
| Liver | Colon, pancreas, stomach, breast, lung |
| Bone | **B**reast, **L**ung, **T**hyroid, **K**idney, **P**rostate (BLT-KP); breast/prostate most common |

**Lytic vs blastic bone mets:**

| Pattern | Cancers |
|---------|---------|
| Lytic | Multiple myeloma, kidney, thyroid, lung, melanoma |
| Blastic | **Prostate**, breast (mixed) |

| Syndrome | Cancer |
|----------|--------|

**Common gotchas:**
- **Stage > grade** for prognosis
- **Lymphoma**, **melanoma**, **glioma**, **mesothelioma**, **seminoma**, **hepatoma** all sound benign but are malignant
- Carcinoma in situ = high-grade dysplasia confined to basement membrane (no invasion)
- "Anaplasia" = total loss of differentiation, marked pleomorphism, abnormal mitoses
- Tumor markers are for **monitoring** (recurrence, response), not screening (with exceptions: PSA, CA-125 controversial)

**Rule of thumb:** Carcinoma = epithelial; sarcoma = mesenchymal. Stage drives prognosis. "-oma misnomers" (lymphoma, melanoma, glioma, hepatoma) are malignant. Bone mets: BLT-KP, with breast/prostate dominating.
